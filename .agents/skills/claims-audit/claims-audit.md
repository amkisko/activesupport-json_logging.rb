# Claims audit

Use when asked to verify, fact-check, or research checkable claims, or when prose under edit states material external facts, quotations, dates, or research summaries.

## Voice

Structured ledger fields stay as specified. Free-form notes stay blunt and evidence-first.

- no sales language or methodology pitches;
- state the fact and move on;
- prefer commas, colons, semicolons, and full stops over em dashes;
- notes destined for `usr/docs/`: plain prose, no markdown tables, bold, italic, or other styling unless the repository explicitly allows it.

## Build a claim ledger

Read the text and record each material claim a reasonable reader could check. Include:

- quotations and attributed paraphrases;
- names, dates, quantities, records, and historical sequences;
- statements about research findings or scholarly consensus;
- technical mechanisms and product behavior presented as fact;
- causal or comparative claims presented as fact;
- claims whose truth is central to the document's purpose.

Combine repeated claims into one ledger entry while preserving every location. Skip ordinary observations and explicit author testimony.

When a working evidence bundle exists, reconcile the ledger with it. Add sources, conflicts, and limitations uncovered during verification. Follow `references/evidence-bundle.md` to create or update that bundle.

## Verify

1. Follow sources already cited by the text.
2. Prefer primary research, official records, standards, original documentation, or direct statements. Use strong secondary sources when a primary source is unavailable or the claim concerns synthesis.
3. Open the source when local copies or time-sensitive facts are missing.
4. Check that the source supports the exact scope, population, date, modality, and causal strength of the prose.
5. Verify quotations against the original and read enough surrounding context to preserve meaning.
6. Check links, identifiers, author names, titles, and publication dates.
7. Search for credible contrary evidence and alternative explanations for central claims. Favorable sources alone are insufficient.

Assign one outcome per claim: `supported`, `partially supported`, `unsupported`, `outdated`, `contested`, or `unverifiable`.

## Repair

Prefer correcting the claim, narrowing its scope, making attribution explicit, or replacing the source. Remove a claim when it adds no necessary work and cannot be supported. Leave interpretive prose interpretive. Leave testimony as testimony.

Default for checkable history, scholarship, quotations, and technical behavior: keep reader-facing provenance. If the user asks to omit source names, still verify any checkable factual residue.

## Report

Return a compact ledger. For each entry: location, claim summary, source, outcome, action. Link to evidence when browsing was required.

Lead with publication-blocking errors, then material qualifications, then accepted unverifiable testimony. Persist the final ledger and unresolved gaps in the evidence bundle when one is in use.

Label every claim not proven by the cited passage as inference, and name the check that would confirm or reject it.
